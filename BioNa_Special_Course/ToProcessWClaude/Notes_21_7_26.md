## 4b. ⚠️ Metadata copy artifacts — three fields propagate when a schedule is duplicated

Copying a schedule in BTS carries stale identity fields into the new test. Confirmed here:

| Field | What it really means | Observed staleness |
|---|---|---|
| `Creator` / `Builder` | who authored the **schedule**, not who ran the cell | CC42–47 inherited `negsa` from CC41 |
| `PN` | schedule creation timestamp | CC42–47 all show CC41's `2026-07-16 17-29-21` |
| `StepName` | the step-file name | **CC36 (test 148) has `BNa_CC35J_20260716.xml`** — copied from CC35 and never renamed |

**Reliable fields** (written per test by the instrument, not copied): `TestID`, `ChlID`, `StartTime`,
`EndTime`, `SCQ`, and the step-file `date` attribute. Use those to identify a run. Treat `Remark`,
`Creator`, `PN` and `StepName` as **hints that may be stale**.

> Same lesson as the filenames: a field can be *present, plausible and wrong*. Corroborate identity with at
> least two independent fields before trusting it.

## 5. Two `.ndax` metadata layouts — parsers must handle both

- **Completed/older exports:** contain `TestInfo0.xml` + `VersionInfo.xml` (+ `Step0.xml`, `EditStep0.xml`).
- **Ongoing/in-progress exports** (CC41–CC47): **no `TestInfo0.xml`** — fall back to **`TestInfo.xml`**.
  A parser that only looks for `TestInfo0.xml` silently skips these seven cells.

Cell identity + mass live in `Step.xml` / `EditStep.xml` (`Remark`, `Creator`, `SCQ`, `SpecificCap`) in
both layouts. `TestInfo*.xml` is encoded **GB2312**, not UTF-8.

## 6. Cycler mass basis — confirmed exactly, and it is NOT ours

Every Neware test: `SpecificCap = 300 mAh/g`, and `SCQ` (active mass, µg) = **(disk − 5.55 mg) × 0.91**,
matching to the microgram on every cell with a known disk mass (CC34–CC40 verified).

Our measured basis is **tare 5.5255 mg** + per-sample **f = 0.882–0.898**.
→ **Recompute all specific capacities from the measured masses.** Nominal C-rates run ~2.4% low
(3C cells); reported mAh/g on the cycler basis is understated by ~2.4%.

`SCQ` also lets you recover a disk mass for any cell whose log entry is missing (as used in §2).
